# PB3 pixel animation

Use Aseprite as the editable source of character art (`/Applications/Aseprite.app/Contents/MacOS/aseprite`, batch CLI + Lua). In PB3, read the repository's `.claude/skills/godot/SKILL.md` before engine changes. The latest hand-off with open issues is `docs/qa/animation-handoff-2026-10-03.md`.

- Inspect native character poses and the current animation before drawing. Preserve the original indexed palette, helmet size, armour shapes and limb proportions. Do not compress a standing pose into a slide.
- Compare the equivalent action of BOTH heroes before inventing a pose. In this project `work/art/reference.lua` extracts Solbrain and `work/art/reference_nova.lua` extracts Nova. Nova's $0D slide is 31–32×16 opaque pixels; his $2C/$2E wall-climb poses are about 15×40 and ladder $1A/$1B about 16×39. These are action references, not universal canvas limits. The rejected Solbrain ladder silhouette was 22×32: broad limbs and a short body made him look inflated. Keep his native helmet and torso scale; raised hands may extend the canvas above his head.
- Measure occupied bounds, torso width, limb thickness and foot/head anchors, not just file dimensions or pixel count. Do not squash or uniformly rescale sprites to fit. Slim armour contours and redraw joints at the native pixel grid; retain a thicker thigh and thinner shin/forearm so slimming does not produce a stick figure.
- Establish key poses and contact points before adding in-between frames. A slide is feet-first with a readable bent supporting leg. Ladder poses alternate opposing arms and legs around a stable torso; keep hand and foot contacts consistent with the rung spacing and movement speed.
- Build arms around three distinct landmarks: shoulder, elbow and wrist. Do not put the elbow on the shoulder–wrist line or obtain it by averaging their coordinates: it reads as a straight stick. Show the bend in the silhouette, not just a joint-colour pixel. During reaching, pulling and recovery, vary upper-arm and forearm angles while preserving plausible lengths and stable hand contacts. Inspect all phases at native size. `work/art/refine_climb_arms.lua` applies this correction only to the saved ladder master's arm layer; `author_traversal.lua` predates it and must not overwrite the corrected master during ordinary exports.
- Work in indexed `.aseprite` documents under `art/solbrain/`, with named animation tags and editable layers. Index 0 is transparent; 1–3 retain their native NES meanings. Do not silently quantize new colours. Keep guides and references out of the exported art.
- Export through `work/art/export_traversal.lua` using Aseprite's batch CLI. `game/data/pb3/sol_traversal.json` is generated output. Keep frame order, dimensions and origins compatible with `SolSprites._traversal`; coordinate animation timing changes with the controller.
- Aseprite masters use the palette actually selected by the gameplay renderer: Solbrain's ordinary sprite palette is indices 17–19 of the NES palette array (zero-based), NOT the second sprite palette. Native Nova uses separate palettes for face/arms and clothing, and suits override them. Keep these meanings explicit in each exporter and reject unsupported indices/opacity.
- Solbrain slide uses a 32×16 canvas, origin (16,16), three frames; the accepted movement has a four-tick entry then a two-frame six-tick loop. The revised ladder uses a 24×48 canvas, origin (12,40), eight phases, four world pixels per phase. Advance it by actual signed travel with camera shifts removed; pause freezes it, descent reverses it. Exclude mount/dismount snaps. Canvas padding must not change hitboxes or move the head/feet anchors.
- Review the loop at native size and nearest-neighbour enlargement, compare with native references, then inspect it in the actual game. Inspect transitions into/out of the move and ladder pause/reverse. Technical tests establish integration, not artistic quality.
- For PB3 traversal changes run the focused movement suite and recorded room routes. Preserve native CHR and unrelated working-tree changes. Do not claim user acceptance from automated checks.
- Save native-size and enlarged loops plus in-game evidence through `work/art/preview_traversal.lua`, `capture_traversal.gd` and `preview_game.lua`. Identify whether evidence is a placed diagnostic or ordinary input from the room entrance. The user accepted the movement on 2026-10-03 but requested slimmer proportions; that acceptance does not cover later shape revisions or future animations.

The user approved direct Aseprite scripting for this workflow. It supports Lua and batch export; no additional plugin is required. Do not introduce an external generation service without a task-specific reason and any required authorization.
- Nova net (Solbrain mesh grab): masters `art/nova/net.aseprite` (8 frames: reach/catch/hold/release, plain and suit), built once by `work/art/author_nova_net.lua` (do NOT rerun over hand edits), decoded with `nova_native.lua`, exported by `work/art/export_nova_net.lua` to `game/data/pb3/nova_net.json`. Export keeps both Nova palettes and splits each 8×16 piece into per-palette overlay sprites; tiles are appended after native PB2 CHR. New drawing numbers (108+) must never index native per-pose physics tables: keep drawing and physical pose separate. Check the 8-sprites-per-line limit for the extra overlays. Focused check: `python3 work/extract/verify_pb3_nova_net.py` (also scan stderr for `SCRIPT ERROR`).
